# Service section — future asset requirements

`section-4.mp4` (90 extracted frames, `assets/source/section-04/service-frames/`)
currently contains two visual contexts, verified frame-by-frame:

- **Frames 1–72** (Inspect, Diagnose, Maintain, Repair): one continuous shot
  in the dealer **workshop** — genuine service activity (diagnostic handset,
  open service panels, filter/fluid checks, tools on the floor).
- **Frames 73–90** (Back to work): a separate outdoor clip — two excavators
  on a gravel/quarry site, driving/working. No technician, no service
  action.

To tell the field-service version of this story — a technician reaching the
machine at its own work site, rather than the machine coming to a workshop —
the project would need new footage. None of the following currently exists
in the asset library, and none should be faked, AI-generated, or simulated
by relabeling the existing clips:

1. **Outdoor inspection footage** — a technician arriving at and visually
   inspecting a machine on an active construction/mining/earthmoving site.
2. **Outdoor diagnosis footage** — a technician using a diagnostic
   handset/tablet on a machine in the field, not in a workshop bay.
3. **Outdoor maintenance footage** — filter/fluid checks, panel access,
   routine servicing performed at the machine's own site.
4. **Outdoor repair footage** — a visible repair action (part replacement,
   tool use) performed at the site rather than in a workshop.
5. **Technician + machine worksite sequence** — a establishing shot showing
   the technician traveling to or arriving at the machine, to visually open
   the "the service team comes to it" beat.
6. **Same-machine continuity footage**, if a field-service narrative is
   produced — one machine, one site, followed through inspect → diagnose →
   maintain → repair → back to work, so the four service stages read as one
   continuous event rather than four separate scenes.

Until this footage exists, the Service section is built honestly on what's
actually available: the workshop clip for the four service stages, and the
outdoor clip only where it genuinely applies (back to work).
